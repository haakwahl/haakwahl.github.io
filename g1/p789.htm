Biografi

Svend Olaus Mathiassen Himo ble født den 3 mars 1832 Himo, Ranum, Overhalla, Nord-Trøndelag. Han giftet seg med Andrea Evensdatter Heg. Han døde den 19 april 1904 på/i Kansas City, Missouri, USA. Han ble gravlagt på/i Oak Hill Cemetery, Lawrence, Douglas, Kansas, USA.
Svend Olaus Mathiassen Himo was also known as Stephan Oliver Himoe. Han ble døpt den 13 juni 1832 på/i Ranem kirke, Overhalla, Nord-Trøndelag. Han emigrerte til USA den 29 mars 1846. Han var utdannet ved lege i 1856 på/i St. Louis Medical College, Missouri, USA. Han.

was serving as the Surgeon of the 6th Kansas Cavalry when the Civil War began.

At the recommendation of his brother-in-law, Colonel Hans C. Heg, Stephen was appointed Surgeon (Feltlæge) of the 15th by Wisconsin Governor Randall on November 11, 1861, with rank from the same date.

Stephen was mustered into Federal service at Camp Randall near the City of Madison, Dane County, State of Wisconsin, on February 28, 1862, to date from February 13, 1862. At the time the Army recorded him as being 29 years old, married, working as a Physician, and residing in Lawrence, Kansas.

As the regiment's Surgeon, Himoe had a small medical staff, including 1st Assistant Surgeon S?J. Hansen, 2nd Assistant Surgeon G. F. Newell and Hospital Steward Anthon O. Oyen.

On September 28, 1863, Surgeon Himoe was ordered to help open the 1st Division Hospital in Chattanooga.

A week later he was ordered to consolidate the Division Hospital into General Hospital No. 2 there.

On October 18th he replied to a letter ordering him to appear before the Army Medical Examining Board at the City of Cincinnati, Ohio, to be considered as a candidate for Surgeon of Volunteers. At some point in October, 1863, the 15th's only remaining doctor, John F. Mehlhorn, died, leaving the regiment without any medical staff. Surgeon Himoe then rejoined the 15th on November 5, 1863, which was then camped near Chattanooga. However, on November 13, 1863, he abruptly resigned from the Army and returned to his family with the Heg's in Waterford.

This was 3 days before he was scheduled to appear before the Army Medical Examining Board.
