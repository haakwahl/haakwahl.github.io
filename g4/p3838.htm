Charles Worthing Freitas

Biografi

Charles Worthing Freitas ble født den 24 november 1942. Han døde i 2022 på/i Claremont, Los Angelses, California, USA.

Chuck Freitas was a generous and selfless supporter of people, be it in his career role as an educator, in his family life, in his engagement with friends, or in his contributions within the community. He asked simply for loyalty, honesty, commitment, and love. In return, the gifts he gave of himself were forever and immense. Born Charles Worthing Freitas on November 24, 1942, to Worthing Charles Freitas and Jordis Astrid Nelson Freitas, Chuck was raised in Orange County and grew up learning from the qualities of strength from his father and gentleness from his mother and a shared commitment to serving others and the community. His father's father---his namesake---owned a small ranch near their home where he could go to ride his horses and learn from his grandfather's wisdom and love. He graduated from Valencia High School where he participated in football, basketball, track, and baseball as well as drama and band/orchestra. He attended Mt. SAC, transferring to La Verne College to complete his undergraduate degree. In 1966, he joined the Claremont Unified School District staff coaching football, teaching first at El Roble Intermediate School and, in 1968, became a Claremont High School teachr in government, economics, and physical education. He became an administrator with CUSD in 1973, developing the Claremont Adult School, which he considered one of his primary career accomplishments. He served as principal at Sycamore and Condit Elementary schools and was recognized for fostering camaraderie and building team among staff and for his positive engagement with parents and his productive, friendly individual involvement with students. He served in numerous supplemental leadership roles at the district, county, and state levels. The Association of California School Administrators was a large part of his professional and personal network and his contributions and leadership were recognized in March 2007 when he received the Robert E. Kelly Award for making a lasting impact on education. In 1993, he was appointed CUSD Director of Student Services and established a partnership with the Claremont Police Department as part of the larger partnership being established between the CUSD and the City of Claremont. Through that partnership, effective joint policies were developed to address the attendance and behavioral needs of Claremont's students. Following his retirement in 2004, he served in several interim positions, including principalships at Sycamore and Condit schools and at a school in Glendora, culminating his career in 2009. In, 2006, Chuck was the recipient of the prestigious Richard S. Kirkendall Award for outstanding contributions to the District and to the Community of Claremont. He was a member of the Rotary Club of Claremont Sunrise from 1988-2015, serving twice as president and helped create the now annual Thanksgiving morning Claremont Turkey Trot. He also served as Director of Literacy Projects for Rotary District 5300 helping to lead the donation of thousands of books for an elementary school in South Africa. He was on the Board of Directors for the Claremont Chapter of the American Red Cross from 1990-2012, serving as President 1995-96 and also served on the boards of Shoes That Fit and The Economy Shop. He was active in his sons' participation in sports, coaching and serving as a board member of the Little League, Pony League, and the American Youth Soccer Organization (AYSO). He served on the City of Claremont Traffic and Transportation Commission from 2010-2016. A highlight of his community service was the honor of serving as Grand Marshal of the City of Claremont Fourth of July parade in 2013. After his wife, Devon's retirement from the CUSD in 2010, the couple enjoyed traveling abroad and in the U.S. Nearly six years ago, they were challenged by his cancer diagnosis.Their immediate decision was "to prove the dire prognosis wrong" and "to make every day a good day." Every day was good day because of the strength of determination and the gentleness of love Chuck and Devon gave themselves, and their family and their friends gave them. And, despite his illness and the pandemic, and because of his stamina, they were grateful to share many wonderful times together in Lake Arrowhead and Palm Desert when they were no longer able to travel publicly. Chuck passed peacefully surrounded and filled with the love of his family and, what he called, his "army of friends" on Saturday, April 16. He is survived by his wife, Devon, their sons Kenneth Freitas (Edith), Michael (Annette) Freitas, Tyson (Jessica) Lingenfelter, and Jeremy (Alicia) Lingenfelter, grandchildren Sarah, Kyle, Benjamin, Paige, Katelyn, Joel, and Reid, his former wife, Beverley Hall, a sister, Judy (Brett) Baker, and nephew, David (Jana) Baker and their children, Ryan and Megan. Published by Daily Bulletin on May 1, 2022.

Han ble gravlagt på/i Oak Park Cemetery, Claremont, Los Angeles County, California, USA.
