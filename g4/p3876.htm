Biografi

Ellsworth Morris Skillingstad ble født den 9 mars 1925 på/i Minnesota, USA. Han døde av av kreft den 17 februar 2006 på/i Mobridge, Walworth, South Dakota, USA. Han ble gravlagt på/i Norway Lutheran Church Cemetery, Mound City, Campbell County, South Dakota, USA.

Stubborn, tough, modest, generous and honest, Ellsworth Morris Skillingstad was a true cowboy of the old West. He loved horses, to play with them and train them. He would much rather harness a team and go feed his cattle than use a tractor, even though the tractor would have been much faster.

He loved rodeos and in his younger days was in them, riding bulls and roping steers. He always said he did a lot of falling off, but his belt buckles and trophies tell a different story and the stories about Stub could fill books. He acquired the nickname Stub for always having a short cigar in his mouth.

He was born raised in rural Glenham. He grew up north of Glenham and attended Knudson School and Mobridge School. Stub did not care for it, transferred to the Glenham School and then decided he could learn more on the ranch. Which is exactly what he did. He went home and worked on the ranch until he entered the U.S. Army and served in Japan.

After his return from service he went back to ranching and in 1958 the family moved north of Mobridge to ranch. Ellsworth was a great teamster. He broke teams for himself and others. He started calf roping in the late 1940s and was well mounted on horses he had trained. He had a roping arena and would have a roping and play day for all the kids. He loved kids and was always there if they needed help, and loved to tease them as he did everyone else.

He used to say, "Horses are just like kids. You have to make 'em mind. It takes a lot of patience and a guy can't be in a hurry."

In Darrell Gill's eulogy at Stub's funeral, he told of the time he and his family were visiting Ellsworth and Cora. "My son was learning to walk. He was sitting on their living room floor and they were encouraging him to take his first step. Ellsworth brought out a mechanical cow that would walk, move its head and beller. Stub wound it up, set it about six feet from Shawn and turned it on. Shawn forgot he couldn't walk, he got up and ran to his mother...and did Ellsworth get a kick out of that!"

"One thing that I admired about Ellsworth is that he never looked back, but always had a positive outlook on life. He did what he wanted to do, when and how he wanted to do it."

Ellsworth was president of the Mobridge Roping Club that sponsored the Little Britches Rodeo. The only time he missed the Mobridge Rodeo was when he was in military service. In 2004 Ellsworth was the Sitting Bull Stampede Grand Marshal. He never missed having his team in the Fourth of July parades and that is the way he rode as Grand Marshall, riding behind the team pulling his wagon.

He loved "rides," the hayrides he gave the Luther League kids from Trinity and the one he always said was the best one of his life - the wagon train that he and Cindy went on with his wagon and team of horses, Pete and Bill. That ride took three days from Strasburg, N.D., to Mound City.
