Karl Johan Hagerup Iversen Storheil ble født den 22 oktober 1868 på/i Storheil, Bindal, Nordland. Han giftet seg med Anne Marie Kristiansdatter Vikestad den 6 februar 1910 på/i Vassås kirke, Bindal, Nordland. Han døde den 3 oktober 1957. Han ble gravlagt på/i Yelm Cemetery, Yelm, Washington, USA.
Karl Johan Hagerup Iversen Storheil had reference number 24741. Han ble døpt den 25 april 1869 på/i Solstad kirke, Bindal, Nordland.

Karl Johan grew up in a lively, bustling household - no doubt his fun-loving nature and his appreciation of a good practical joke stemmed from his upbringing. Although there was certainly time for levity, life for the Johnsen household was filled with countless chores and much hard work. In addition to cultavating the rocky soil of Storheil which grew wheat, barley, and potatoes; Iver Mikael also kept a flock of sixteen sheep, six cows, and one horse, according to the census records for 1875.

Storheil is nestled at the foot of a jagged granite mountain-cliff, the outline of which - with just a little imagination - looks like the profile of an old bearded man lying on his back! Because of this somewhat unusual silhouette,

the mountain is affectionately called "Bjørnson" by local residents. Although somewhat sheltered from behind, Storheil is vulnerable to the wind from the sea in front. From the shores of Lysfjorden, just a stone's throw away from the farm, one can see the Norwegian Sea low on the horizon. The ever-present wind even on a clear warm day can be rather cool, causing one to magine what the gales of a fierce winter storm must be like.

Boarding the Canadian liner "Tasso" on jul 18, 1900, Karl Johan made his way to Hitterdal, Minnesota. It is possible that there was an enclave of others from Bindal living in this part of the country, making the transition of settling in foreign country considerably easier.

Although Norwegian census record indicate his name to be "Karl Johan Hagerup Iversen", US immigration and naturalization records for 1927 show his name to be "Carl John Hagerup Iverson", reflecting a certain degree of anglicization in the spelling of his name. While still in Norway he was known as "Karl Iversen" or "Karl Storheil", however once in North America he more commonly just used "Carl Iverson".

Within a month after his arrival at Washington, Carl filled a Declaration of Intention with the naturalization Service

stating:

I, Carl John Hagerup Iverson, aged 52 years, occupation; Farmer, do declare on oath that my personal description is Color; white, Complexion; fair, Height; 5 feet 7 inches, Weight; 194 pounds, Color of hair; brown, Color of eyes; blue, Other distinctive marks; none. I was born in Brønnøy, Norway on the 22nd day of October, anno Domino 1868; I now reside at Yelm, Washington. I emigrated to the United States of America from Buchanan, Saskatchewan, Canada. I am married; the name of my wife is Anna Marie; she was born at Brønnøy, Norway and now resides at Yelm, Washington. It is my bona fide intention to renounce forever all allegiance and fidelity to any foreign prince, potentate, state, or sovereignty, and particularly to George V, King of Great Britain and Ireland, of whom I am now a subject...

By november 7, 1927 Carls subsequently filed for a Petition for Naturalization, and after meeting all necessary requirements was sworn as a US citizien at the Thurston County Superior Court in Olympia on October 4, 1929.

Anna remained a subject of Norway until her death,

Taking the local steamship down the coast, Karl Johan arrived by mid-July in Trondheim - his port of embarkation to the adventures awaiting him beyond the Atlantic.

Still a bachelor and thoughts of finding a wife became increasingly important to him. While on a visit to Canada a

friend from Norway named Oluf Jakobsen mentioned to Carl that his fiance'e's elder sister was still single. Known for her gentle and quiet nature, and hard work, the name of Anna Kristiansen Vikestad was discussed. This wad not an unfamiliar name to Carl for his father came from Vikestad, and it was known that Kristian Kornelius Mkkelsen hade lovely daughters. Once the idea of her eligibility had become a real possibility, Carl began correspondence with Anna. Prior to his return to Norway, he might have even sent an old-fashioned "frier-brev"

of letter of proposal. Carl journeyed back to Bindal in December 1909 and arrived just before Christmas time.

The fire event may have contributed to Carl's decision to move his family from Canadam which meant starting all over again. By this time, he had become a citizien of Canada, and no doubt had had the intentions of remaining there permanently. Plans began to meterialize for a move back to the United States - this time not to the familiar territory of Minnesota, but to the Pacific Northwest. Non November 17, 1920 Carl and his family boarded the Great Northern Railway for the two-day trip to Washington State. Arriving through Blaine, Washington Carl, Anne and their four children pulled into the Tacoma Railway Station on November 19th.

Having heard of the fertile soil of the Yelm prairie, Carl had his heart set on berry farming, and soon began to make the necessary preperations. While Anna and the children stayed in Tacoma with his nephew, Ibenhart Eriksen, Carl purchased 15 acres of property. With the help of friends a home was built and the fields properly prepared for berry farming.

Utvandret til Canada og USA. Bosatte seg senere i Seattle og har mange etterkommere der.
